How I write and check these guides

Updated 27 November 2026

I am Sajjal Bajaj, an ERP consultant and project manager based in Mohali, India. These are independent guides for people planning and operating business systems. My CV describes my background, and the case studies explain selected project contributions and their attribution.

What kind of evidence supports a guide?

I distinguish three things: behaviour described by the software provider, my recommendations for implementation, and illustrative examples. A recommendation explains what I would check or how I would define acceptance. It should not be read as a built-in software control unless a cited source establishes that behaviour.

For product instructions, the relevant official documentation is the starting point. Version, edition, localisation, hosting and custom modules can change the result. A documentation check is not a live test of your database. Where I describe a completed test, it needs a clear environment and observed result.

How should you use examples and case studies?

Illustrative numbers, records and scenarios demonstrate a method. They are not claims about a client, a measured improvement or a test I performed. Case studies have their own status and qualifications. Where a project result is attributed to a published project account, it is not presented as my own measurement.

Use the ERP acceptance evidence worksheet to turn a recommendation into a test for your own circumstances. Record the expected outcome and compare it with what actually happens.

What do review dates mean?

The publication date records when an article was first dated. An updated date records a substantive change to that article. A source-review note identifies which references were checked and when; it does not certify every configuration or promise that a provider will leave the feature unchanged.

Some older guides have received structural changes before a full factual review. Historical release previews are context for their original publication period and should not be used as current feature specifications. Read the version and limitations in the article before following a step.

How are writing tools used?

AI assistance has been used to draft and organise parts of this library. That does not make generated wording evidence. Technical claims need sources, examples need clear labels, and errors need correction. Repeated generic sections are being replaced with focused explanations or links to a shared method.

What is outside the scope of these articles?

A software workflow does not establish legal, accounting, tax, employment, clinical or privacy compliance. The relevant specialist must define those requirements. The articles can help translate an agreed requirement into configuration and acceptance questions.

How can you report a correction?

Email sajjalbajaj@gmail.com with the article address, the statement or step concerned, your software version and a source or reproducible description of the issue. Please remove credentials and confidential information. I can then investigate the specific claim and update the guide where needed.

Clear sourcing and useful explanations help readers assess the work. They do not guarantee that a search engine or AI service will rank, recommend or cite it.
