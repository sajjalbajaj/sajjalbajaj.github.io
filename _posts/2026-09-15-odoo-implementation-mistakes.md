Most Odoo implementations that go badly do not fail because the software is wrong. They fail because of a handful of
avoidable mistakes made along the way. Having seen a fair few projects, here are the ones that come up again and again,
and how to steer clear of them. This kicks off a short series on common Odoo mistakes.

## Customising before understanding the standard

The single most common mistake is customising too early. A business sees that Odoo does something slightly differently
from their old system and immediately asks to change it, before they have understood why Odoo works the way it does.
Very often the standard approach is fine, or better, once you learn it. Every customisation you avoid is money saved
and a smoother upgrade later.

## Trying to launch everything at once

Ambition is good, but going live with every module on day one is risky. Teams get overwhelmed, problems in one area
spill into others, and it is hard to tell what is going wrong. Phasing the rollout, core first, then the rest, gives
people time to adjust and makes issues easier to isolate.

## Skipping the data cleanup

Businesses often import years of messy data straight into a shiny new system, duplicates, half-finished records and
all. The new system then inherits the old mess. Cleaning data before import is unglamorous but decisive; it is the
difference between trusting your new system and fighting it.

## Underinvesting in training

People make or break a system. When training is treated as an afterthought, staff fall back on old habits, work around
the system, and the data suffers. A little proper training turns reluctant users into confident ones, and confident
users are what make the whole project pay off.

## No clear owner

Projects without a clear internal owner drift. Decisions stall, scope creeps, and the implementation partner is left
guessing. Someone on the business side needs to own the project, make decisions and keep it moving. That single role
matters more than most people expect.

## Treating go-live as the finish line

Go-live is the start, not the end. The first weeks reveal what training missed and what needs adjusting. Teams that
plan for a settling-in period, with support on hand, come out far better than those who declare victory and walk away.

## Practical tips

- Learn the standard before you customise; change only what truly needs changing.
- Phase your rollout rather than launching everything at once.
- Clean your data before import, not after.
- Invest in training; it protects everything else.
- Name a clear internal project owner.
- Plan for support after go-live, not just up to it.

## My take

None of these mistakes are about the software; they are about how the project is run. The good news is that means they
are all avoidable. Learn the standard, phase the rollout, clean the data, train the people, own the project and support
it after launch, and most of what derails Odoo projects simply never happens.
