Construction runs on projects, sites, subcontractors and tight margins, and when each of those is
tracked separately, money quietly leaks. Continuing the series on industry problems and how ERP solves
them, here is construction.

## Problem: you cannot tell if a project is profitable

The classic construction problem: costs land in different places, and by the time you add them up, the
project is finished and the margin is whatever is left. A healthy overall business can hide loss-making
jobs.

**How Odoo solves it:** analytic accounting tags every cost and revenue to a project, so you see the
real profitability of each job, live, not months later. Loss-makers surface while you can still act.

## Problem: materials are scattered across sites

Materials sit at different sites and stores, and without a clear view you over-order in one place and
run short in another, tying up cash and delaying work.

**How Odoo solves it:** multi-location inventory tracks stock by site, so you know what is where, move it
sensibly, and stop buying what you already have on another job.

## Problem: subcontractors and procurement are hard to control

Between subcontractors, suppliers and purchase orders across multiple projects, it is easy to lose track
of what was ordered, received and owed.

**How Odoo solves it:** purchasing tied to projects keeps every order, receipt and bill linked to the
right job, so procurement stays controlled and costs land against the correct project.

## Problem: progress billing and cash flow are messy

Construction cash flow depends on billing as work progresses, and getting that wrong strains the whole
business.

**How Odoo solves it:** with invoicing linked to your projects and orders, you can bill accurately as
work is delivered and keep a clear view of what has been invoiced and what is owed.

## Problem: no single view of the business

Estimating, purchasing, site work, accounts, all in separate tools means no one sees the whole picture.

**How Odoo solves it:** one connected system brings projects, procurement, inventory and accounting
together, so everyone works from the same information and nothing falls through the cracks.
