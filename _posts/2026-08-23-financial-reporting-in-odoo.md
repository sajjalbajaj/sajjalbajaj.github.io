All the invoices, bills and reconciliation add up to one thing: reports that tell you how the business is
really doing. Odoo produces the core financial statements automatically and in real time, which is a genuine
advantage. Here are the key reports, explained without the accountancy jargon.

## The profit and loss (income statement)

The profit and loss report answers the question everyone asks: are we making money? It shows your income,
your costs, and the profit or loss left over, for a period you choose.

Reading it tells you not just whether you profited, but where the money went: how much came from sales, how
much went on materials, wages, overheads. It is the report you look at most often to understand performance.

## The balance sheet

The balance sheet answers a different question: what is the business worth right now? It shows what you own
(assets), what you owe (liabilities), and the difference (your equity).

Where the profit and loss covers a period, the balance sheet is a snapshot at a moment. Together they give
you both the flow and the position of your finances.

## The cash picture

Profit is not the same as cash, and plenty of profitable businesses hit trouble by losing sight of cash.
Odoo helps you see your cash position and what is owed to and by you, so you can manage the money that
actually keeps the lights on, not just the accounting profit.

## The big advantage: real time

Here is what makes reporting in Odoo powerful. Because your invoices, bills, payments and stock all feed the
accounts as they happen, your reports are live. You are not waiting weeks for your bookkeeper to close the
month before you can see how you did.

That changes how you run the business. You can spot a problem, a slipping margin, a cash squeeze, while there
is still time to act, rather than reading about it long after the fact.

## Digging deeper

Beyond the headline statements, Odoo lets you drill into the detail, from a report down to the transactions
behind a number, and combine financial reports with the analytic view to see profitability by project or
line. The reports are a starting point for questions, not just a printout.

## Practical tips

- Look at your profit and loss regularly, not just at year end.
- Watch cash alongside profit; they are not the same thing.
- Use the ability to drill into the numbers when something looks off.
- Combine financial and analytic reporting to see not just the result, but where it came from.

## My take

Financial reporting is where all the bookkeeping pays off in understanding. Odoo gives you the profit and
loss, balance sheet and cash view in real time, so you always know where you stand. Use them often, watch
cash as well as profit, and drill in when something surprises you. Live numbers you trust are the foundation
of good decisions.
