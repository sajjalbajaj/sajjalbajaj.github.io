Once you have decided to move to an ERP, the next question is where it should actually live: in the
cloud, or on your own servers. It sounds like a technical detail, but it shapes your cost, your
control and your day-to-day maintenance for years. Here is a plain-English guide to choosing well.

## The three options, briefly

Most businesses are really choosing between three setups:

- **Cloud (hosted by the vendor):** the provider runs everything. You just log in and use it. With
  Odoo, this is Odoo Online.
- **Managed cloud platform:** hosted in the cloud but with more room to customize and control. With
  Odoo, this is Odoo.sh.
- **On-premise (self-hosted):** you run the software on your own servers, or your own cloud account,
  and your team looks after it.

The right answer depends on how much control you need, how much you want to maintain, and the rules
your industry plays by.

## Cloud ERP: the case for it

For most small and mid-sized businesses, cloud is the sensible default.

- **Lower upfront cost:** no servers to buy, a predictable subscription instead.
- **Less to maintain:** updates, backups and security patches are handled for you.
- **Fast to start:** you can be up and running quickly, with no hardware to procure.
- **Access anywhere:** your team can work from wherever they are, on any device.
- **Scales easily:** add users or resources without buying new kit.

The trade-off is less low-level control. You work within the platform's boundaries, and your data
lives with the provider (a reputable one, with strong security, but their servers, not yours).

## On-premise ERP: the case for it

On-premise still makes sense for specific situations.

- **Full control:** over the environment, the customizations and exactly when you update.
- **Data residency:** the data physically sits on infrastructure you own, which some regulations or
  policies require.
- **Deep customization:** heavy, unusual customizations are sometimes easier when you own the stack.
- **Works offline:** useful if your site has unreliable internet.

The trade-off is real responsibility. You need the hardware, the IT skills and the discipline to
handle backups, security and updates yourself. Miss those, and the "control" quietly becomes risk.

## How to choose: a few honest questions

- **Do you have in-house IT to run servers well?** If not, cloud removes a burden you would struggle
  to carry.
- **Do regulations dictate where your data must live?** If yes, that may point you to a specific
  setup, so check first.
- **How heavy are your customizations?** Standard needs suit cloud. Very deep, unusual builds may
  favour a managed platform or on-premise.
- **What is your appetite for maintenance?** Cloud trades some control for a lot less upkeep. Be
  honest about which you actually want.
- **How predictable do you need costs to be?** Subscriptions are steady and predictable. Owning
  hardware means bigger upfront spend and occasional refresh costs.

## My take

For the large majority of small and mid-sized businesses, cloud ERP is the right starting point.
You get up and running faster, spend less upfront, and hand the maintenance headaches to people who
do it for a living. On-premise earns its place when you have genuine control, compliance or
customization reasons, and the IT capability to back it up.

The mistake is choosing on-premise for a feeling of control you will not actually use, and then
carrying the maintenance load anyway. Match the hosting to your real needs and capabilities, not to
a preference on paper.
