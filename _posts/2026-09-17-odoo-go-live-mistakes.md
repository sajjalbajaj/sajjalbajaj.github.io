Go-live is the moment a project becomes real, and it is also where good projects can stumble at the last step. The
software may be ready, but launching badly can still make the first weeks painful. To round off this series on common
Odoo mistakes, here is go-live.

## Launching without real testing

Skipping proper testing to hit a date is a false economy. Bugs and gaps that a test phase would have caught instead
surface live, in front of frustrated users, when the stakes are highest. Test with real scenarios and real data before
go-live, so launch day holds few surprises.

## Treating go-live as the finish line

Go-live is the start of a new phase, not the end of the project. The first weeks reveal what training missed and what
needs tuning. Teams that plan for a settling-in period, with support ready, ride out the bumps; teams that declare
victory and disband leave users stranded exactly when they need the most help.

## Pulling support too soon

Related, and worth stating on its own: the days right after go-live are when questions peak. Having support readily
available then, and reducing it gradually as confidence grows, makes an enormous difference to how people feel about the
new system. Withdraw too soon and small problems become lasting resentment.

## No fallback plan

Going live with no plan for what to do if something goes seriously wrong is a gamble. A sensible cutover plan, with a
way to keep the business running if there is a problem, turns go-live from a leap of faith into a managed step. Hope is
not a strategy on launch day.

## Ignoring the human side

A new system is a big change for the people using it, and treating go-live as purely technical misses that. Clear
communication, encouragement and patience with people learning new ways of working matter as much as the software.
People who feel supported adopt the system; people who feel dropped work around it.

## Practical tips

- Test with real scenarios and data before you go live.
- Treat go-live as the start of a new phase, not the finish line.
- Keep support strongest in the first weeks, easing off gradually.
- Have a fallback plan so a problem does not stop the business.
- Look after the people, not just the technology.

## My take

Go-live is where preparation pays off or shortcuts catch up with you. Real testing, a settling-in period, ready support,
a fallback plan and attention to the people are what turn a launch into a smooth transition rather than a rocky few
weeks. The software being ready is only half of it; landing it well is the other half.

This series has walked through the mistakes that most often trip up Odoo projects, across implementation, the core apps,
data and go-live. Almost none of them are about the software; they are about how the work is done. Avoid them, and Odoo
does what it is meant to. I will keep sharing more from real projects over time.
