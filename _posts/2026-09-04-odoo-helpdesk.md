Customer support handled through a shared inbox quickly becomes chaos: messages missed, duplicated replies,
no idea what is outstanding. Odoo Helpdesk brings order to it. Continuing the series on Odoo Services and
Project, here is helpdesk.

## Everything becomes a ticket

In Helpdesk, each customer request becomes a ticket, a tracked item with a status, an owner and a history.
Instead of issues living in an inbox where they can be overlooked, every request is captured and visible
until it is resolved. Nothing quietly falls through the cracks.

## Teams and pipelines

You can organise support into teams and move tickets through stages, so it is always clear what is new, in
progress and resolved. Agents know what to pick up next, and managers can see the state of support at a
glance rather than guessing.

## SLAs keep promises

If you promise response or resolution times, Helpdesk can track them with service level agreements, so you
see what is at risk of breaching and act in time. That turns support promises from good intentions into
something you actually measure and meet.

## Context in one place

Each ticket holds the conversation and the relevant details, so whoever picks it up has the full story. And
because Helpdesk is part of Odoo, support connects to the customer's wider record, so agents are not working
blind.

## Learn and improve

Because support is tracked, you can see the patterns: common issues, response times, workload. That lets you
fix recurring problems at the source and staff support sensibly, rather than firefighting the same things
forever.

## Practical tips

- Capture every request as a ticket, so nothing is missed.
- Organise into teams and clear stages so work flows.
- Use SLAs to track and meet your response promises.
- Watch the patterns and fix recurring issues at the root.

## My take

Helpdesk turns support from a stressful scramble into an organised, measurable process where every request
is tracked and every promise is visible. Because it lives in Odoo, support sees the whole customer, and you
learn enough to actually improve. For any business serious about service, that structure is essential.
