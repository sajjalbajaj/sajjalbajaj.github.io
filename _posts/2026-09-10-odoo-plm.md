Products are not static. Designs change, components get swapped, improvements are made, and in manufacturing
those changes have to be controlled or chaos follows. Odoo PLM, Product Lifecycle Management, brings order to
that. Continuing this run through more Odoo apps, here is PLM.

## The problem PLM solves

In a business that makes things, the bill of materials, the recipe for a product, changes over time. Without
control, different people work from different versions, changes get made without proper review, and the shop
floor builds from outdated information. PLM exists to manage those changes properly.

## Engineering change orders

The heart of PLM is the engineering change order: a controlled way to propose, review and approve a change to
a product or its bill of materials. Instead of someone quietly editing the recipe, a change is documented,
reviewed and approved before it takes effect. Everyone ends up working from the same, agreed version.

## Versions under control

PLM keeps track of versions, so you know which version of a product is current and can see how it evolved. If
a change causes a problem, you can understand what changed and when. That traceability is valuable for quality
and for accountability.

## Better collaboration on changes

Product changes usually involve several people: design, production, purchasing. PLM gives them a shared,
structured way to collaborate on a change, rather than trading emails and hoping everyone is aligned. The right
people review the change before it hits the floor.

## Connected to manufacturing

Because PLM sits with Odoo Manufacturing and Inventory, approved changes flow into the bills of materials that
production actually uses. So the control PLM provides is not theoretical; it directly keeps the shop floor
building the right thing.

## Practical tips

- Manage bill-of-materials changes through controlled change orders, not ad-hoc edits.
- Keep versions tracked so you always know what is current.
- Involve the right people in reviewing changes before they take effect.
- Let approved changes flow into the BoMs production uses.

## My take

PLM matters most to manufacturers whose products evolve, which is most of them. It turns product change from a
risky free-for-all into a controlled, reviewed, traceable process, keeping bills of materials accurate as
designs move on. For anyone making things, that control protects both quality and sanity.
