Most businesses are sitting on plenty of data and very little insight. The numbers live in different
systems, reports arrive too late, and nobody is quite sure which figure to trust. A good dashboard
fixes that: one clear, live view of what matters. Here are five I would set up for almost any growing
business.

## What makes a dashboard actually useful

Before the list, one principle. A dashboard is not a wall of charts. It answers a specific question
at a glance, uses live data instead of last month's export, and points to an action. If a chart does
not change a decision, it does not belong there. Keep each one focused.

## 1. The sales dashboard

The question it answers: are we winning enough work, and where does it stand?

Show pipeline by stage, deals won and lost, revenue against target, and who or what is driving it.
This is where sales leaders should start their day. It turns a vague sense of "how are we doing" into
a clear picture.

## 2. The finance dashboard

The question it answers: is the business healthy?

Revenue, expenses, margin, cash flow and outstanding invoices. Cash flow especially, because plenty
of profitable businesses run into trouble simply by losing sight of it. A live finance view beats
waiting for the month-end report.

## 3. The inventory and operations dashboard

The question it answers: can we deliver what we sell?

Stock levels, items running low, slow movers, and order fulfilment status. For anyone holding stock,
this is the difference between smooth delivery and either stockouts or cash tied up in the wrong
products.

## 4. The customer and service dashboard

The question it answers: are our customers happy and staying?

Open support tickets, response and resolution times, repeat customers and churn. Winning a customer
is expensive, so keeping the ones you have is one of the highest-value things to watch.

## 5. The leadership overview

The question it answers: how is the whole business doing, on one screen?

A handful of headline numbers pulled from the others: revenue, cash, sales, a key operations metric
and a customer metric. This is the one for owners and managers who need the big picture without
digging through detail.

## How to actually build these

You do not need a huge project. The practical path:

1. **Pick one dashboard**, usually sales or finance, whichever hurts most.
2. **Agree the handful of numbers** that genuinely matter. Resist the urge to show everything.
3. **Connect it to live data** from your ERP, CRM or accounting system, so it updates itself.
4. **Put it where people will see it** and use it in real decisions.

Tools like Power BI are built exactly for this: pull data from your systems, turn it into clear,
live visuals, and share them with the people who need them.

## My take

The goal is not prettier charts. It is faster, better decisions from data you already have. Start
with one dashboard that answers a real question, connect it to live data, and build from there. Once
your team feels the difference between guessing and knowing, they will never want to go back.
