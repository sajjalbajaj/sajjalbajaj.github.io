Starting a fresh Odoo database can feel like staring at a blank page. Where do you begin? The answer is the same every
time: get your company basics right first, because everything else builds on them. This kicks off a short series of
practical Odoo how-to guides.

## Start with your company details

The first thing to set is your company itself: name, address, tax registration numbers, phone and email. These are not
just labels; they appear on your invoices, quotations and official documents, so getting them right matters from day
one. You will find these under the general settings, in the company section.

## Set your currency and language

Your company's main currency underpins all your financial figures, so set it before you start entering anything with a
price. Likewise, set the language and regional formats so dates, numbers and addresses appear the way your business and
country expect. These are quick to set and awkward to change once data exists.

## Add your logo

Upload your company logo early. It appears on the documents customers actually see, quotations, invoices, order
confirmations, so a proper logo makes your business look professional from the first document you send. It is a small
step with a visible payoff.

## Install only the apps you need

Odoo lets you install apps as you need them, and the temptation is to switch on everything at once. Resist it. Start
with the apps for what you are doing now, and add more as you grow into them. A focused set of apps keeps the system
simpler to learn and manage.

## Decide single or multi-company early

If your business is really several companies, decide how you will model that before you go far, because it shapes a lot
of the setup. Most businesses are a single company and can keep it simple. If you genuinely need multi-company, plan it
deliberately rather than switching it on halfway through.

## Practical tips

- Fill in complete, correct company details; they appear on your documents.
- Set your main currency and regional formats before entering data.
- Upload your logo so customer-facing documents look professional.
- Install only the apps you need now, and add more later.
- Decide single versus multi-company early, before setup goes deep.

## My take

The company setup is unglamorous, but it is the foundation the whole system rests on, and small things left wrong here
show up on every document and report later. Spend a little time getting the basics right, company details, currency,
logo, a sensible starting set of apps, and the rest of your Odoo journey starts on solid ground.
