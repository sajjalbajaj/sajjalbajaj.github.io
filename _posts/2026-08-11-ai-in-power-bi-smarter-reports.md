Power BI has quietly become one of the most approachable places to use AI on your business data. You do
not need to be a data scientist. Several AI features are built right in, and used well they turn a pile
of numbers into answers much faster. Here is a practical tour of what they do.

## Ask questions in plain English (Q&A)

The Q&A feature lets anyone type a question, "sales by region last quarter," and get a chart back,
without building it by hand. It is one of the simplest ways to let non-technical colleagues explore
data safely, and it is a genuine time saver for quick questions that would otherwise land in your inbox.

## Copilot: your reporting assistant

Microsoft's Copilot brings generative AI into Power BI. You can describe the report or page you want in
words and let it draft the layout, ask it to summarise what a report is showing, or get help writing
DAX, the formula language, without wrestling the syntax yourself. It does not remove the need to
understand your data, but it removes a lot of the blank-page and boilerplate effort.

## Find what is driving the numbers (Key Influencers)

The Key Influencers visual analyses your data to show what actually moves a metric. Which factors drive
higher customer churn? What is linked to bigger deal sizes? Instead of guessing, you get the main
influencers ranked for you. It is one of the most underused, genuinely insightful features in the tool.

## Break it down (Decomposition Tree)

The Decomposition Tree lets you explore a number by breaking it down dimension by dimension, and it can
use AI to suggest where to look next, automatically drilling into the biggest contributors. It turns
"why is this number off?" into a few clicks instead of a morning of pivoting.

## Automatic written summaries (Smart Narratives)

Smart Narratives generate a written, plain-language summary of what a report shows, and the text updates
as the data does. It is perfect for readers who want the story in words, not just charts, and it saves
you writing the same commentary every month.

## Spot the odd ones out (Anomaly Detection)

On time-series charts, Power BI can automatically flag anomalies, unexpected spikes or dips, and even
suggest possible explanations. Instead of scanning lines for something odd, you get the outliers
highlighted for you, which is invaluable for monitoring.

## Using these well: a few tips

The features are powerful, but the old rules still apply:

- **Clean, well-modelled data first.** AI insights on messy data are misleading. A good data model is
  the foundation.
- **Treat AI as a starting point.** Let it surface influencers and summaries, then apply your judgement
  and business context before you act.
- **Keep reports focused.** AI does not excuse a cluttered dashboard. A clear question per page still
  wins.
- **Check before you trust.** Verify that a suggested driver or anomaly makes real-world sense.

## My take

The AI in Power BI is not a gimmick. Q&A opens data to everyone, Copilot speeds up the building, Key
Influencers and the Decomposition Tree find the "why," and Smart Narratives and anomaly detection do the
watching and the writing. Used on clean, well-modelled data, they get you from numbers to decisions
noticeably faster. The tool is smarter than ever, but your understanding of the business is still what
makes the insight count.
