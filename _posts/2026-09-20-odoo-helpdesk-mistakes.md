Support is where you keep the customers you worked hard to win, so mistakes in the helpdesk cost you loyalty as well as
time. Most come from tickets that fall through gaps and support that runs disconnected from the rest of the business.
Continuing this series on common Odoo mistakes, here is Helpdesk.

## Letting tickets fall through the cracks

The cardinal sin of support is a request that no one picks up. When tickets have no clear owner or no follow-up, they
languish and customers feel ignored. Every ticket should have an owner and a next step, so nothing sits unanswered.
Odoo makes that easy; the mistake is not enforcing it.

## No structure or priorities

Treating every ticket the same means urgent problems wait behind trivial ones. Without stages, priorities and
assignment, the team works reactively and important issues slip. A little structure, priorities, clear stages, so
everyone knows what needs attention first, is what keeps support on top of the queue rather than buried under it.

## Support cut off from the customer record

When helpdesk runs disconnected from sales and contacts, agents lack context: who this customer is, what they bought,
their history. Because Odoo links it all, support can see the full picture. Skipping that connection means agents work
blind and customers repeat themselves, which nobody enjoys.

## Not using a knowledge base

Answering the same questions over and over is wasted effort. A knowledge base lets you answer common issues once and
point people to it, freeing the team for the problems that genuinely need them. Teams that skip this stay stuck handling
the same tickets forever.

## Ignoring the metrics

Response times, resolution times, ticket volumes, they tell you whether support is actually working and where it
strains. Running a helpdesk without watching these means you cannot tell if customers are being served well until they
leave. Use the reporting to manage support deliberately, not by gut feel.

## Practical tips

- Give every ticket an owner and a next step so none get lost.
- Use stages and priorities so urgent issues come first.
- Connect support to the customer record for full context.
- Build a knowledge base for the questions you answer repeatedly.
- Watch response and resolution metrics to manage support well.

## My take

Helpdesk mistakes cost you the customers you already have, which makes them expensive in a quiet way. Clear ownership,
sensible priorities, connected context and a knowledge base are what turn support from a source of frustration into a
reason customers stay. Run it deliberately, with the metrics in view, and support becomes a strength rather than a leak.
