An Odoo implementation succeeds or fails long before you touch the software. After running
plenty of them, here is the checklist I actually use, from first conversation to a smooth
go-live. Work through it roughly in order.

## 1. Before you start

- Define the outcome you want in business terms, not a list of features.
- Name a clear project sponsor and decision maker.
- Set a realistic budget and timeline, and protect a contingency.
- Pick a small, high-value first phase instead of trying to do everything at once.

## 2. Map your processes

- Document how each core process actually works today: sales, purchase, inventory, accounting.
- Note the real pain points, and separate the must-haves from the nice-to-haves.
- Decide where you will adopt Odoo's standard way of working instead of customizing.

## 3. Clean your data

- Identify your master data: products, customers, suppliers, chart of accounts.
- Deduplicate and standardise it before migration. Dirty data in means dirty data out.
- Agree opening balances and stock counts for the cut-over.

## 4. Choose the right setup

- Community or Enterprise edition, based on the features you truly need.
- Hosting: Odoo Online, Odoo.sh, or on-premise.
- Confirm the module list for phase one, and keep it tight.

## 5. Configure before you customize

- Set up companies, users, roles and access rights.
- Configure the standard features first and see how far they take you.
- Only build custom modules for genuine gaps, and document every one.

## 6. Plan integrations early

- List the systems Odoo must talk to: e-commerce, payments, shipping, banking.
- Decide what needs to be real-time versus batch.
- Test integrations with real data, not just samples.

## 7. Test properly (UAT)

- Build test scripts from your real processes.
- Have actual end users run them, not only the project team.
- Log issues, fix them, and retest until the critical path is solid.

## 8. Train and prepare people

- Train users on their real daily tasks, inside the system.
- Write short, role-based guides they will actually use.
- Manage the change: explain why, and bring people along early.

## 9. Go live with a plan

- Choose a sensible cut-over date and freeze the old system.
- Load final data and opening balances.
- Have support on hand for the first few busy days.

## 10. After go-live

- Watch adoption closely and fix friction fast.
- Hold a short review: what worked, what to improve.
- Plan phase two once phase one is stable.

## My take

Most Odoo projects that struggle skipped the boring parts: process mapping, data cleanup and
testing. Do those well, keep customizations lean, and go-live becomes a non-event, which is
exactly what you want.
