One of the best reasons to run your business on Odoo is that all the data sits in one place, ready to report on. Yet
many businesses barely use that, or use it badly. Continuing this series on common Odoo mistakes, here is reporting and
dashboards.

## Not using the built-in reporting

The most common mistake is exporting everything to spreadsheets to analyse, ignoring the reporting Odoo already
provides. Sales, inventory, finance and more come with live reports and pivot views built in. Reaching for a spreadsheet
by reflex means redoing work the system would do for you, on data that is already out of date the moment you export it.

## Trusting reports built on dirty data

A report is only as good as the data beneath it. Duplicate contacts, miscategorised transactions and inconsistent
records produce confident-looking reports that are simply wrong. Before trusting the numbers, trust the data; reporting
and data quality are two sides of the same coin.

## Measuring everything, deciding nothing

Some teams build elaborate dashboards packed with every metric imaginable and then act on none of them. A dashboard is a
tool for decisions, not decoration. Focus on the few numbers that actually drive action in your business, and let the
rest go. A cluttered dashboard hides the signal that matters.

## Dashboards nobody looks at

A dashboard set up once and never opened is wasted effort. Reporting only helps if it is part of how you actually run
the business, reviewed regularly and used in decisions. Build the habit of looking, or the finest dashboard is just a
screen nobody visits.

## Not tailoring reports to the audience

The number a warehouse manager needs is not the one the finance director needs. Showing everyone the same generic report
serves no one well. Tailor what each person or team sees to the decisions they make, and reporting becomes genuinely
useful rather than a one-size-fits-nobody wall of figures.

## Practical tips

- Use Odoo's built-in reports and pivots before reaching for spreadsheets.
- Keep the underlying data clean so reports can be trusted.
- Focus dashboards on the few numbers that drive decisions.
- Review reporting regularly so it shapes how you run the business.
- Tailor reports to what each audience actually needs.

## My take

Reporting is where the value of having everything in one system is finally cashed in, and it is often left on the table.
Using the built-in tools, trusting clean data, focusing on decision-driving numbers and actually looking at them are
what turn Odoo from a system that records the business into one that helps you steer it. That shift is worth making.
