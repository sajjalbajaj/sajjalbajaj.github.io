Odoo Inventory is one of the most capable apps in the whole suite, and one of the most
misunderstood. People think of it as a simple stock counter, but it is really a full warehouse
management system. This guide gives you the big picture before we dig into the details in the rest
of this series.

## What Odoo Inventory actually is

At its heart, Odoo Inventory tracks what you have, where it is, and how it moves. But it goes much
further than a running total. It manages lead times, automates replenishment, and lets you design
advanced routes for how goods travel through your business. If you hold stock of any kind, this is
the engine that keeps it accurate.

## The four areas to understand

The app organises itself into four broad areas, and once you see them, everything else falls into
place.

### 1. Product management

This is the foundation: how you define your products. It covers product types, units of measure and
packaging, plus the tracking methods that follow individual items, like lot numbers, serial numbers
and expiration dates. Getting this right up front makes everything downstream cleaner.

### 2. Warehouses and storage

This is where your stock lives. It covers warehouses, internal locations and operation types, along
with replenishment strategies like reordering rules and make-to-order, and the reporting that tells
you what you actually hold.

### 3. Shipping and receiving

This is stock in motion: goods coming in and going out. It handles inbound and outbound flows,
delivery methods and carriers, how stock is reserved, and the strategies for picking and removing
items, such as FIFO, LIFO and FEFO.

### 4. Inventory valuation

This is the money side. It links your physical stock to your accounts, covering valuation methods,
landed costs and how the value of what you hold shows up in your books.

## Why it matters for your business

The reason Inventory sits at the centre of so many Odoo projects is simple: stock touches almost
everything. Sales cannot promise what you do not have. Purchasing needs to know when to reorder.
Accounting needs the value of your stock. Manufacturing consumes it. When your inventory is accurate
and automated, the rest of the business runs on solid ground. When it is not, everything wobbles.

## How to approach it

Do not try to switch on every feature at once. The sensible path is to nail the basics first: define
your products cleanly, set up your warehouse and locations, and get everyday receipts and deliveries
flowing accurately. Once that foundation is solid, layer in reordering rules, advanced routes and
valuation. That is exactly the order we will follow through this series.

## My take

Odoo Inventory rewards a bit of upfront thought. Treat it as a warehouse management system, not just
a stock list, and set up the foundations properly, and it becomes one of the most powerful parts of
your ERP. Rush it, and you inherit the messy data that undermines everything else.
