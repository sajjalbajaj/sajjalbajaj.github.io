For services businesses, projects are where the money is made or lost, and Odoo Project can show you which, if you use
it well. The common mistakes here are quiet ones that erode margins without anyone noticing. Continuing this series on
common Odoo mistakes, here is Project.

## Not tracking time honestly

The single biggest project mistake is time that goes unrecorded or gets guessed at later. Without honest timesheets you
cannot know what a project actually cost, so you cannot know if it made money. Build the habit of logging time as work
happens; it is the raw data everything else depends on.

## Disconnecting projects from billing

When project work and invoicing live apart, billable time slips through the cracks and revenue leaks. Odoo can turn
tracked time and milestones into invoices directly. Keeping projects connected to billing means you actually charge for
the work you did, rather than discovering later that you undercharged.

## No visibility on profitability

Many teams run projects with no live view of whether they are in the black. By the time the truth emerges, the project
is over and the loss is locked in. Use Odoo's project profitability view to see how you are doing while there is still
time to act, not as a post-mortem.

## Vague task structure

Projects run as a loose pile of tasks with no clear stages, owners or deadlines drift and stall. A sensible structure,
clear stages, an owner per task, realistic dates, keeps work moving and makes status obvious at a glance. Structure is
not bureaucracy; it is what keeps a project honest.

## Ignoring planning and capacity

Committing to work without checking whether the team has capacity leads to overload and missed deadlines. Using planning
to see who is available and how loaded they are turns commitments into something realistic. Promising what you can
actually deliver protects both margins and reputations.

## Practical tips

- Track time honestly as work happens; it underpins everything.
- Keep projects connected to billing so no billable work is lost.
- Watch project profitability live, not after the project ends.
- Give tasks clear stages, owners and deadlines.
- Use planning to commit only to what the team can deliver.

## My take

Project mistakes rarely announce themselves; they show up as margins that are thinner than expected. Honest time
tracking, connected billing, live profitability and a bit of structure are what turn projects from a hopeful guess into
a managed, profitable activity. For a services business, that discipline is the difference between busy and profitable.
