"How much does Odoo cost?" is the question I hear first, and the honest answer is that it
depends on your setup. But it is not a mystery. Odoo cost comes down to three parts, and once
you understand them you can estimate and control it.

## The three parts of Odoo cost

1. **Licensing** (the software itself)
2. **Implementation** (getting it set up)
3. **Ongoing** (running it over time)

## 1. Licensing

Odoo comes in two editions. The **Community** edition is open source and free to use. The
**Enterprise** edition is a paid subscription, priced per user, with more apps and official
support included. Odoo also offers a plan where a single app is free. Exact prices vary by
region and plan, so check the official Odoo pricing page for current numbers. The key point is
this: for most businesses, licensing is not the biggest cost.

## 2. Implementation

This is usually the largest and most variable cost, and it is where a good partner earns their
fee. It covers discovery and process mapping, configuration, any custom development, data
migration, integrations and training. A small, standard implementation is modest. A large,
heavily customized rollout across many modules and users costs a lot more. Scope is the main
lever here.

## 3. Ongoing

Budget for subscription renewals, support and maintenance, enhancements over time, and extra
users or apps as you grow. Hosting is part of this too: Odoo Online is included with Enterprise,
Odoo.sh is a managed platform with its own fee, and self-hosting means your own server and admin
costs.

## What actually drives the number

- The number of users.
- The number of modules, and how standard your needs are.
- The amount of customization.
- Data quality and migration complexity.
- Integrations with your other systems.

## How to keep the cost sensible

- Start with a lean first phase, not everything at once.
- Use Odoo's standard features wherever you can.
- Keep customizations disciplined and documented.
- Clean your data before migration.
- Choose your partner on value and fit, not just day rate.

## My take

For most small and mid-sized businesses, Odoo is one of the most cost-effective serious ERPs
available. Licensing is rarely the big number. Implementation scope is. Control the scope, keep
customizations lean, and you control the cost.
