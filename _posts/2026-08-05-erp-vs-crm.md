Two of the most common questions I get are "what is the difference between ERP and CRM?" and "do I
need both?" They sound similar, they often overlap, and plenty of vendors blur the line on purpose.
Here is a clear, jargon-free answer.

## The short version

- **CRM** looks outward, at your customers and sales.
- **ERP** looks inward, at how your business runs.

Both manage important information, but they point in different directions. Get that straight and the
rest falls into place.

## What a CRM does

CRM stands for **Customer Relationship Management**. It is the system your sales, marketing and
support teams live in. Its job is to help you win and keep customers.

A CRM typically handles:

- Leads and opportunities, and where each one sits in your pipeline.
- Contact and account history: every call, email and meeting.
- Sales activities, quotes and follow-ups.
- Marketing campaigns and, often, customer support tickets.

In short, a CRM is about the **relationship**: who your customers are, what they need, and what
happens next to move a deal forward.

## What an ERP does

ERP stands for **Enterprise Resource Planning**. It is the backbone that runs your operations, the
system of record for how work actually gets done.

An ERP typically handles:

- Finance and accounting.
- Inventory, purchasing and the supply chain.
- Order fulfilment and, for makers, manufacturing.
- HR, projects and reporting across the business.

Where a CRM is about winning the customer, an ERP is about **delivering** to them and running the
business behind the scenes.

## Where they overlap

The confusion usually starts at the sales order. A deal that a salesperson closes in the CRM has to
become a real order that operations fulfil in the ERP. That handover, from "we won it" to "we
deliver it," is exactly where the two worlds meet.

This is why many modern platforms include both. Odoo, for example, offers CRM and ERP as part of
one connected suite, and Microsoft and Salesforce come at it from their own strengths. The details
differ, but the goal is the same: one smooth flow from a lead to a delivered, invoiced order.

## Do you need one, or both?

It depends on where your pain is.

- **If your challenge is winning and keeping customers,** disorganised leads, missed follow-ups, no
  visibility on your pipeline, start with a **CRM**.
- **If your challenge is running the business,** messy inventory, finance in spreadsheets, orders
  falling through cracks, start with an **ERP**.
- **If both hurt,** you likely need both, ideally connected so information flows between them instead
  of being re-keyed.

You do not have to buy everything at once. Solve the most painful problem first, then connect the
next piece when you are ready.

## The real goal: one connected picture

The point of either system is the same: stop important information living in scattered spreadsheets
and inboxes, and put it somewhere shared and trustworthy. The magic happens when your customer side
and your operations side talk to each other, so a won deal becomes an order, an order becomes a
delivery, and everyone is working from the same facts.

## My take

CRM wins and keeps customers. ERP runs the business that serves them. Most growing companies end up
needing both, but the smart move is to start with the one that hurts most, prove the value, and
connect the rest over time.
