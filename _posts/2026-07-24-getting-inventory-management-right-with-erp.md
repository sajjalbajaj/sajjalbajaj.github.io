Inventory is where a surprising amount of money hides. Hold too much and cash sits on the
shelf. Hold too little and you lose sales and disappoint customers. Getting inventory right
is one of the fastest ways an ERP pays for itself, so here is how to think about it.

## When spreadsheets stop working

Spreadsheets are fine until you have multiple products, locations or people updating stock at
once. Then numbers drift, two people sell the same unit, and nobody trusts the count. That is
the signal to move inventory into a proper ERP.

## What good inventory management looks like

- **Real-time stock levels** that everyone sees, across every location.
- **Automatic reorder rules (min/max)** so you restock before you run out.
- **Lot and serial number tracking** for traceability and recalls.
- **Accurate stock valuation** that flows straight into your accounts.

## Reorder rules beat gut feel

Ordering "when it looks low" causes both stockouts and overstock. Reorder rules that account
for real demand and supplier lead times keep the right amount on hand automatically. This
alone frees up cash that would otherwise sit in excess stock.

## Accurate stock means accurate everything

Inventory accuracy is not just a warehouse concern. It drives correct order promises, clean
accounting and reliable reports. Wrong stock numbers quietly corrupt decisions across the
whole business.

## Common mistakes I see

- No cycle counting, so errors pile up until a painful year-end stocktake.
- Ignoring lead times when setting reorder points.
- Keeping separate stock lists per team instead of one source of truth.

## How ERP helps

Modern ERP like Odoo handles multi-location stock, reorder rules, lot and serial tracking and
valuation out of the box, with everything tied to sales, purchase and accounting. The software
is the easy part. The value comes from clean data and disciplined processes.

## My take

Start with clean, accurate stock data, set sensible reorder rules, and run regular cycle
counts. Do that, and inventory stops being a guessing game.

Fighting your inventory? That is exactly the kind of problem a well-configured ERP solves.
