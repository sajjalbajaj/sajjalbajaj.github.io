Point of Sale is where the customer meets your business, so mistakes here are felt immediately, by staff mid-sale and by
customers waiting. Most trace back to setup and to treating the till as separate. Continuing this series on common Odoo
mistakes, here is Point of Sale.

## Treating the till as an island

The advantage of Odoo POS is that sales, stock and accounting all update from the same tills. Running the POS as a
standalone cash register throws that away. Sales should reduce stock and post to accounts automatically, so set it up as
part of the connected system rather than a separate box on the counter.

## Messy product and pricing setup

A slow, error-prone checkout usually comes down to product setup: missing barcodes, wrong prices, badly organised
categories. Staff then hunt for items and make mistakes while customers wait. Clean products, correct prices and a
sensible layout are what make the till fast and accurate.

## Not planning for offline

Shops lose connectivity, and a POS that stops working when the internet drops stops the business. Odoo POS is designed
to keep working offline and sync later, but only if you understand and set that up. Plan for it, so a network blip does
not become a queue of stuck customers.

## Ignoring stock accuracy

If POS sales do not keep stock accurate, you end up selling things you do not have and reordering blind. Because the
till feeds inventory, keeping that link working means your stock reflects what actually sold. Accurate stock is one of
the biggest reasons to run POS on your ERP in the first place.

## Skipping the reporting

Every sale through the POS is data: what sells, when, at which location, by which staff member. Ignoring that reporting
means missing the patterns that would help you stock and staff better. The till is not just taking money; it is telling
you how the shop is doing, if you look.

## Practical tips

- Run the POS as part of your connected system, not a standalone register.
- Keep products, barcodes, prices and categories clean for a fast checkout.
- Set up and understand offline mode before you need it.
- Keep the POS-to-inventory link working so stock stays accurate.
- Use POS reporting to see what sells and staff accordingly.

## My take

Point of Sale is unforgiving of setup shortcuts because problems appear live, at the counter. A connected till, clean
products, a working offline plan and accurate stock are what make it smooth for staff and customers alike. Run it as
part of the whole system and the POS does far more than take payment; it keeps the business in step with every sale.
