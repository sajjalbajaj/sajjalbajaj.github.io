# Search and AI content standard

Adopted 27 November 2026. Applies to future blogs, supporting resources and substantive revisions.
This is an editorial workflow, not a claim that a format guarantees search or AI visibility.

## Choose the task before the title

Prepare a private topic brief containing:

1. The reader, observable problem and decision the content will help resolve.
2. Demand evidence, including report type, dates, filters and sample limitations where available.
3. Existing pages that answer the same intent, and the decision to refresh, expand or create.
4. The contribution beyond a documentation summary: a diagnostic path, reproducible worked example,
   supported comparison, original worksheet or evidenced experience.
5. Exact official sources, required version/edition/provider checks and unresolved questions.
6. The intended action or useful next resource for a reader.

Keep property metrics, full exports and research notes in gitignored `content-review/`. Never publish
them accidentally in front matter or commit them with a public planning document.

## Prefer depth over duplicate coverage

Update the existing guide when the problem and outcome are the same. A different keyword order,
location name or version number is not sufficient reason for another article. A version-specific
article must explain a material supported difference. A new resource should let readers do something
the existing guide does not already provide.

Do not generate a page for every related query that an AI system might use. Search demand can identify
useful needs without dictating a quota. Existing schedules remain unchanged unless separately updated.

## Write for the person doing the work

Answer the question clearly, then explain the relevant conditions and sequence. Use headings, tables
and short answers where they improve understanding. There is no ideal length or required tiny-chunk
format. Do not pad an article or repeat generic rollout advice to reach a word count.

For troubleshooting, connect the symptom to competing causes, the evidence that distinguishes them,
the appropriate next action and the result to verify. Preserve safeguards around irreversible changes.
For comparisons, define the alternatives and the conditions under which the choice changes.

Use British spelling and no em or en dashes. Keep technical claims next to specific official sources.
Label scenarios illustrative or proposed unless an actual test or result is evidenced. Do not invent
personal experience, clients, savings, quotations, credentials or statutory assurances.

Use relevant original diagrams, authorised screenshots or official videos when they explain a task.
Write descriptive alternative text and link to the source where appropriate. An outbound reference
is not a backlink to this website; do not manufacture mentions, reviews or authority signals.

## Preserve the technical and trust foundations

Use the established author, editorial policy, canonical and metadata layouts. Visible FAQs and their
schema come from the same front matter; do not add duplicate Article or speculative AI schema blocks.
Keep substantive content available as readable HTML and link it from relevant guides and subjects.

FAQs are for useful follow-up answers. Google retired FAQ rich results in May 2026; keeping valid
FAQPage markup does not promise a Google FAQ display, special AI eligibility or ranking benefit.

Use real modification dates and keep original URLs and publication dates for revised articles.
Verify internal destinations are available by publication. Confirm source pages contain the claimed
instructions, including following redirect or refresh stubs to the actual document.

The existing llms.txt file can remain a maintained directory for systems that use it. Google's
guidance says it does not use these files to improve visibility or ranking in Search. It is not a
substitute for crawlable, indexable, useful pages or permission controls.

## Validate and measure honestly

Run strict structure and focused exact-prose checks for new articles. Review facts, novelty, overlap,
metadata and media separately; automated checks cannot establish those qualities.

Measure Search clicks, impressions, CTR and position separately from the dedicated Google generative
AI impression report. That report covers Google AI features, not every AI assistant. Do not add its
impressions to overall Search totals, or describe impressions as clicks, enquiries or unique people.

Compare equal date windows with the same filters after enough complete post-publication data exists.
Record reporting delays and uncertainty. Do not attribute old impressions to a later content change.
No new reporting or posting automation is created by this document.
